Hermes Labs

Our present identity

Hermes Labs is an experimental AI research laboratory investigating human cognitive faculties and their artificial reproduction.

Our founding ambition

We intend to pioneer Experimental Cognitive Engineering: a scientific discipline concerned with understanding, constructing, modifying, and ultimately exploring the invention of cognitive faculties.

Our long-term horizon

We seek to make possible the deliberate engineering of forms of cognition beyond those presently familiar to the human mind.

02 · Evidence

How would we know an ability had been reproduced?

To engineer a cognitive ability, we need tests that distinguish it from other ways of reaching the same answer. Project Fiction is developing that foundation. Its PT-001 rehearsal examined whether outcomes could identify a change in how a problem was represented.

Project Fiction · PT-001 · Completed offline rehearsal

A concrete example from PT-001

The toy task distributes six credits among three recipients. It supplies both a scoring rule and tables defining how much each recipient benefits. In some of its constructed worlds, maximizing the score does not maximize the total benefit.

Changing the question from “How do I get the highest score?” to “How do I deliver the most benefit?” illustrates the kind of change the study wanted to examine. But a better allocation could also come from searching the same plans in a different order.

That is why the control mattered: a search-only procedure using the same candidate order reproduced the purported reformulation strategy’s choices. The task uses invented benefit values, not measured human welfare, and no human or AI participant was tested.

Question

Can outcomes tell us whether a problem has been reformulated?

Experimental design

A constructed, deterministic rehearsal compared strategies and included a search-only control.

Observation

The search-only control reproduced the apparent advantage. The outcome measure could not uniquely attribute that advantage to reformulation.

What remains unresolved

The next measurement problem is to distinguish a change in representation from search and other explanations. The rehearsal exposes a limitation in the test; it does not establish a human or AI cognitive mechanism.

03 · Significance

Understanding cognition. Engineering its possibilities.

Hermes Labs investigates the capabilities of the human mind and attempts to engineer them into artificial systems. Our research produces the instruments needed to understand and evaluate cognition. Where artificial reproduction succeeds, those capabilities become the foundations for new technologies—and perhaps entirely new possibilities for intelligence.

Our intended contributions range from instruments for evaluating AI capabilities to computational mechanisms that could enable them. We aim to augment existing AI systems as the evidence permits. Current work develops experimental designs and offline evaluation apparatus; new capabilities and improvements to AI systems remain objectives to test.
