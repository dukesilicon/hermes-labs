Project Affect

How does affect regulate what a cognitive system attends to and does?

A cognitive system cannot attend to everything or pursue every possibility. What makes information urgent, an outcome valuable, or a risk worth avoiding? Project Affect investigates whether affect-associated regulatory functions can be reconstructed in artificial systems.

Working scopeExplore the questionNext questionsResearch records

Working scope

Emotion and affect as regulatory mechanisms of cognition: shaping attention, salience, valuation, memory, motivation, risk perception, and action selection.

Unresolved boundaries

The target is measurable synthetic regulation. Emotional self-description does not establish regulation, biological equivalence, or subjective feeling. A first regulatory target and execution priority remain unresolved.

Regulation that changes cognition

Illustrative scenario · Not an executed experiment or adopted protocol

Consider two artificial systems with access to the same information. One allocates more attention to uncertain hazards; the other prioritizes a potential reward. If their decisions differ, the investigation would ask which regulatory policy caused the difference and how changing that policy changes behavior.

For frontier AI, describing an emotion and using affective language are readily observable outputs; a regulatory mechanism requires a different account. It must be specified, measured, and perturbed, with consequences for attention, valuation, memory, motivation, risk perception, or action selection. This illustration does not establish an affective mechanism, subjective feeling, or correspondence with human affect.
