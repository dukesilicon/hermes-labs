# Project Daydream — Research question

Status: unresolved

## Current working formulation

How should spontaneous internally generated cognition without immediate external task demand be characterized, what makes it productive, and can that productive off-task cognition be meaningfully instantiated and evaluated in AI models?

This is a concept-formation question. It leaves open whether a defensible computational analogue can be specified and measured.

## Priority open questions

1. What do "spontaneous", "internally generated", and "without immediate external task demand" mean, and what external conditions remain compatible with the target?
2. How does the proposed phenomenon differ from deliberate imagination, mind-wandering, spontaneous thought, generative search, and stochastic model behavior?
3. What observable evidence would distinguish a relevant cognitive process from output diversity or randomness alone?
4. What information, state, task, or temporal boundaries would an operationalization need to specify?
5. What would justify a claim of a computational analogue, and what would make the framing too ambiguous or unmeasurable to pursue as currently stated?
6. When are associative drift, mind-wandering, incubation, or cross-domain connections productive, and what evidence distinguishes useful idea emergence from off-task activity alone?

## Resolution status

No precise definition, measurement scheme, mechanism, or architecture is adopted. The relationship to Project Imagination and Project Fiction remains an open issue, not a basis for merging the programs. Record any adopted boundary in [research status](research-status.md), [methodology](methodology.md), and the research log.
